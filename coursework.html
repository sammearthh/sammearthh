This course develops clear professional communication through writing, non-verbal communication, business reports, discussions and presentations. These summaries and key concepts are based on the five uploaded teaching handouts.

01

Communication, Types and Barriers

+

Communication creates shared understanding through messages, suitable channels and feedback. This module examines the process in face-to-face, digital and hybrid settings.

Key concepts from the notes

  1. The communication process

    Sender, encoding, message, channel, decoding, receiver and feedback work together. Noise can disrupt meaning at any stage.

  2. Choosing a channel

    Synchronous communication supports immediate discussion. Asynchronous messages allow people to respond later and preserve a written record.

  3. Audience and context

    Language, formality and medium should fit the audience, purpose and situation. Check individual preferences when communicating across generations.

  4. Barriers and gateways

    Jargon, assumptions, distraction, notification overload and missing tone can obstruct understanding. Plain language and clarification help reduce these barriers.

  5. Active listening

    Pay attention, ask open questions, paraphrase and summarise before responding. On video calls, reduce distractions and make turn-taking clear.

Possible application

In B2B appointment setting, a useful application would be to summarise the prospect's requirement and confirm the agreed date and next step in writing.

02

Types of Non-Verbal Communication

+

Meaning also travels through posture, eye contact, voice, space, timing and appearance. The notes extend these ideas to online meetings and digital messages.

Key concepts from the notes

  1. Body, eyes and space

    Kinesics concerns body movement, oculesics concerns eye behaviour, and proxemics concerns space. Interpret cues within their cultural and situational context.

  2. Voice and delivery

    Paralanguage includes pitch, pace, volume, tone and pauses. A measured pace and purposeful pauses can make an explanation easier to follow.

  3. Touch, time and appearance

    Haptics, chronemics and artifacts describe how touch, timing and surroundings communicate. Respect boundaries and consider the professional setting.

  4. Digital body language

    Camera framing, punctuation, emojis and response timing influence how an online message is received. Make intentions explicit when tone could be unclear.

  5. Interpreting evidence carefully

    The handout limits Mehrabian's percentages to the specific conditions of the research. Context and clusters of cues matter when interpreting another person.

Possible application

For the introduction video, a practical application is to place the camera at eye level, use steady eye contact, keep the background clear and rehearse pauses.

03

Business Writing - Emails, Messages and Memos

+

Professional writing combines audience awareness, clear structure, appropriate tone and a specific action. The notes cover letters, emails, workplace messages, memos and responsible AI-assisted drafting.

Key concepts from the notes

  1. The seven Cs

    Check that a message is clear, concise, concrete, correct, coherent, complete and courteous. Include the facts the reader needs to act.

  2. Put the request early

    BLUF means Bottom Line Up Front. A specific subject line, brief context, clear request and next step help a busy reader respond.

  3. Adapt the structure

    Routine requests and good news can use a direct approach. Sensitive messages need thoughtful sequencing, clear reasons and respectful wording.

  4. Choose the right format

    Match the channel to urgency, complexity and the need for a record. Memos use To, From, Date and Subject fields for internal communication.

  5. Use AI with judgement

    P-A-C-T specifies Persona, Audience, Context and Task. Review generated text for factual accuracy, tone and personal voice before using it.

Possible application

A prospect follow-up could briefly refer to the previous conversation, state the purpose of the appointment and offer clear meeting options.

04

Business Reports - Structure, Style and Data Storytelling

+

Reports organise evidence around a decision. This module covers report types, structure, objective writing, effective charts and executive summaries supported by verified data.

Key concepts from the notes

  1. Define the report's purpose

    Establish the audience, question and scope before drafting. An analytical report interprets evidence and can recommend action.

  2. Build a traceable structure

    Connect the introduction and methodology to findings, conclusions and recommendations. Put supporting data and calculations in appendices when appropriate.

  3. Make charts communicate

    Select a chart suited to the question, label units and dates, include a source and use a title that communicates the finding.

  4. Write an executive summary

    Summarise purpose, key findings, conclusion and recommendations. Write it after the report so every claim can be traced to the main text.

  5. Verify AI-assisted analysis

    R-A-F-T-S specifies Role, Audience, Facts, Task and Shape. Recalculate numerical claims and check that suggested explanations are supported by evidence.

Possible application

For a future music project, a short feasibility report could compare equipment costs, audience needs and possible revenue using sourced figures and clearly stated assumptions.

05

Speak, Brand and Pitch

+

This module brings together discussions, presentations, LinkedIn, web writing, public speaking, crisis communication, storytelling and pitching innovations.

Key concepts from the notes

  1. Contribute to discussions

    Build arguments with Point, Evidence, Explain and Link (PEEL). Listen to others, respond respectfully and support the group's purpose.

  2. Structure spoken answers

    PREP organises an answer as Point, Reason, Example and Point. For questions, practise Listen, Pause, Rephrase and Answer.

  3. Show a professional identity

    A useful profile combines role, focus, proof and goal. Support strengths with genuine work and keep your professional identity consistent across platforms.

  4. Write for digital audiences

    Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs and meaningful links. Adapt the expression of a message to its platform and give the reader a clear next action.

  5. Tell a story and make an ask

    An elevator pitch connects a hook, identity, problem, solution, proof and request. Crisis communication requires acknowledging the issue and communicating verified next steps.

Possible application

A music venture pitch could introduce the audience's problem, explain the proposed idea, show available evidence and finish with a specific request for collaboration.

MODULE VOCABULARY

Words for professional communication

Definitions from the course notes with suggested practice sentences to personalise.

Feedback

A response that helps check whether a message was understood.

Please share feedback on the proposal before our next meeting.

Paralanguage

The vocal features that accompany speech, including tone, pace and pauses.

A steady pace and clear pauses would improve the paralanguage in my introduction.

BLUF

Bottom Line Up Front, placing the main point early.

Using BLUF, I would begin my follow-up email with the meeting request.

Executive summary

A short, self-contained account of a report's purpose, findings, conclusion and recommendations.

The executive summary should explain the project's findings and proposed next steps.

Call to action

The specific next step a message asks its audience to take.

My portfolio ends with a call to action inviting visitors to connect with me.
